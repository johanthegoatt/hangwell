How it works

Galleries hang art so the middle of the picture sits at eye level, about 145 cm (57 inches) off the floor, whatever the size. The catch is the nail: the wire or hook sits a little below the top of the frame and lifts it, so the nail goes above the middle by half the frame height minus the wire drop. Hangwell does that sum, so you mark the nail straight off the floor.

For a row of frames, line them up by their middles, not their tops, so different sizes still read as a set. Hangwell puts every frame's middle on the eye-level line (so the nails land at different heights) and spreads the row across the wall with equal gaps and equal space at the ends. If the frames are wider than the wall, it says so instead of running them off the edge.

A wide or heavy frame hangs straighter on two hooks, a quarter of the way in from each edge, so it can't tip and is easy to level. And the fixing depends on the wall: a light frame hangs on a hardened picture hook, but plasterboard needs a proper toggle for anything heavier, and a stud or brick holds far more. The 57-inch centre is common museum practice; fixing weights follow the usual trade bands — always check the pack's own rating.

Hangwell is a guide for hanging pictures, not a structural calculation. For something very heavy, a mirror or a TV, use fixings rated for the weight and find the studs or use the right masonry anchors. Nothing you type leaves your device.
